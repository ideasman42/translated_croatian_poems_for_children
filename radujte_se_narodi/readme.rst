#################
Radujte se narodi
#################

*English translation "Rejoice, You Nations".*

:Written by: Unknown
:Date: circa 1827

The best-loved Croatian Christmas carol: the news of Christ's birth, and the call to Bethlehem.

Notes
=====

A traditional carol, the one every Croatian knows, sung at Midnight Mass and through
Christmas. Out of copyright. It was first printed by the Franciscan Marijan Jaić in
*Vinac bogoljubnih pisamah* (1827), with its tune in his *Napivi bogoljubnih
cérkvenih pisamah* (1850); the usual harmony is Franjo Dugan's.

Each verse's last two lines are sung twice.

*Glas* is "voice", and so "news", as here. *Porodi* is "was born", an older form;
*pristupljuj* is an old imperative, "draw near". *Otajstvo* is a holy mystery.
*Uboštvo* is "poverty": Bonaventura Duda notes that many mis-sing it as *ubojstvo*
(murder). *Štalica* and *slamica* are fond diminutives of *štala* (stable) and
*slama* (straw), kept as "little". *Jaslice* is the manger, and the Christmas crib.

Text as in fra Bonaventura Duda's commentary in *Svijeta razveselitelj* (bitno.net),
matching *Život* (1990), *Forum* (1997) and prayer books of 1861 and 1895 in the
main. Some have "i Betlemu" or "i k Betlehemu" for "i k Betlemu", "štuj, štuj"
(honour) for the second "čuj, čuj", and "Glorija". Duda gives a fourth verse,
"Pođi i ti, narode, betlehemskom dvoru", which most sing without.
