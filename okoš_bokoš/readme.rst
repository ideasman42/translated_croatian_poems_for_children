##########
Okoš bokoš
##########

*English translation "Okoš Bokoš".*

:Written by: Unknown
:Date: circa 1965

A fist-counting game with a rude second line, which children love for exactly that reason.

Notes
=====

A traditional fist-counting game: everyone holds out their fists, and the counter taps
each in turn to the beat. The child landed on at the end names a number, and the
counting goes on for that many more taps; the fist it stops on is put away, and a child
with both fists gone is out. Out of copyright.

The traditional wording is kept: *prdne* is "farts". Kindergarten versions clean it up,
to *skoči kokoš* (the hen jumps) or *pita kokoš* (the hen asks); *Vijenac* 437
(Matica hrvatska) notes the folk original is "prdne kokoš", and that it is often
censored.

"Okoš bokoš" is nonsense, made to rhyme with *kokoš* (hen), kept as it is.
*Baja* is a folk word for an older brother or a fellow, or a nickname; treated here
as a name. It is in the nominative (the object would be *baju*), so it is Baja who
asks; the English puts him before the verb, since "asks Baja" would read as the hen
asking him. *Kol'ko* is the spoken form of *koliko* (how many).

Text from Lukin portal za djecu (pjesmicezadjecu.com, "Dobre stare igre"), with the
majority wording of the Kreativna kućica blog, Mama Lisa and a rhyme collected in
Varaždin in 2007. Line 3 is also found as "pita Paja" (a name) or "pita kume".
