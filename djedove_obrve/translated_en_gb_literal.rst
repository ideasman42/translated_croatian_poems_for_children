######################
Grandfather's Eyebrows
######################

| My grandfather has unusual eyebrows.
| When he grows angry with me,
| they turn green like grass.
| When he does not believe me, they turn yellow like a lemon
| full of pips, and when he wonders
| at my quick answer,
| like a boat on the sea
| they sway, rise
| and fall, up-down,
| down-up, and I go red
| and on the table I begin
| to shift the cups about.
| But, luckily, grandmother, seeing how matters stood,
| sits down by us, takes the newspaper and reads
| what is happening in the world,
| who has it hard, who has it easy.
| Then her eyebrows too rise up, higher and higher,
| and she mutters slowly: well, well...
| But grandfather's eyebrows keep still, he is serious.
| He says nothing and his face is hard.
| Only when he falls asleep do I see: his
| eyebrows are white as snow
| and on them are resting
| two weary doves
| ready at any moment to fly away
| somewhere far off.
