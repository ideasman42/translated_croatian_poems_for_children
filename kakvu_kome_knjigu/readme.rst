#################
Kakvu kome knjigu
#################

*English translation "What Book for Whom".*

:Written by: Grigor Vitez

If the animals went to school, each would need its own kind of book.


Translation
===========

- ``translated_en_gb_literal.rst`` keeps as close to the Croatian as English allows

Notes
=====

A question and answer for each animal, and a promise at the end: carp growing on
an oak is the Croatian for "never", like pigs flying.

The animals' names are made up like surnames: *medo bundović* from *bunda* (a fur
coat), "Furcoatson"; *zeko zeković*, "Bunnyson"; *sovo noćobdijo* (owl who stays up
at night); *rače natražnjače* (crab who goes backwards). *Goluban* is a big or
cock pigeon. *Leći se* is to be born or hatched, of animals. *Listovi* are both the
pages of a book and the leaves of a plant, so the bunny's book is good to eat.

*Naške* is "of our kind", rhyming with *unatraške* (backwards); the meaning is
taken from the context.

The poem rhymes in couplets (*kolegu* / *legu*, *red* / *med*); the rhymes are not
kept.

**Text from one source only**, the "Škola" page of Lukin portal za djecu
(pjesmicezadjecu.com). The poem is Vitez's: it is in his *Izabrane pjesme i priče*
(1973) and was translated into Russian. No date was found.
