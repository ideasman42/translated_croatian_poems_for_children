###############
Zec i vjeverica
###############

*English translation "The Hare and the Squirrel".*

:Written by: Ratko Zvrko
:Date: circa 1967

The bob-tailed hare envies the squirrel her tail; she tells him to mind his own.

===========

Notes
=====

From *Grga Čvarak* (Mladost, 1967), Zvrko's best-known book: poems about the boy
Grga Čvarak and the animals and people around him, long a grade-2 lektira
(required reading).

*Kusat* is "short-tailed" or "bob-tailed", as a hare is. *Zeko* is the fond word
for a hare, "bunny".

*Reko*, *ko* and *poso* are the spoken forms of *rekao* (said), *kao* (like) and
*posao* (work); *za poso se brini svoj* is "take care of your own work", as
"mind your own business". *Svak* is *svatko*, "everyone".

*Čim* in line 10 is the instrumental of *što*, "with which", not "as soon as";
*prednjačiti pred drugima* is "to be ahead of others", so "which puts them ahead
of others".

The poem rhymes in couplets (*zeko* / *reko*, *rep* / *lijep*); the rhymes are not
kept.

Text from the 4th edition of *Grga Čvarak* (Mladost, 1974), matching Lukin portal
za djecu (pjesmicezadjecu.com), which writes the spoken forms with apostrophes
(*rek'o*, *k'o*, *pos'o*, *Svak'*). Line 9 ends with a full stop in the 1974 copy
(a scan, read by OCR), with the next line in lower case; it is taken to be a comma,
as on Lukin portal. The date is that of the book's first edition; the poem is
confirmed only in later editions.
