##############
Ježić Ubodežić
##############

*English translation "Little Hedgehog Pricklet".*

:Written by: Luko Paljetak

A little hedgehog out on a hill runs home when it starts to snow, and goes to bed.

Notes
=====

Nearly every line ends in *-ežić*, the small, fond ending of *ježić* (little
hedgehog): *briježić* (little hill), *snježić* (little snow). Paljetak makes up the
rest: *Ubodežić* from *ubod* (a prick or sting), *Bježić* from *bježati* (to run
away) and *Ležić* from *ležati* (to lie). The English makes them up the same way,
with "-let". *Snježić* is kept as "little snow", a pet name, not "a little snow",
which would mean a small amount.

*Peo se* is "was climbing", from *peti se*, a variant of *penjati se*; *padat* is
the clipped infinitive of *padati* (to fall); *obuko* is the spoken form of *obukao*
(put on). *Spava li spava* is "sleeps and sleeps", "sleeps soundly".

Paljetak's poem for the letter J in Profil Klett's grade-1 primer *Moji tragovi 1*.
First printed in the magazine *Stražilovo* (Novi Sad), no. 18, between 1970 and
1974; the year is not known.

**Text from one source only**, Lukin portal za djecu (pjesmicezadjecu.com, "Stihovi
za 10"); no printed copy was found to check it against. The portal starts every line
with a capital letter, which may be its own style.
