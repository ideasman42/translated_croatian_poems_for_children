###############
Nadmudrena lija
###############

*English translation "The Fox Outwitted".*

:Written by: Gustav Krklec
:Date: 1952

The fox asks the pheasant why it sits so high; the pheasant tells her.

Notes
=====

From *Telegrafske basne* (Telegraph Fables, 1952): fables of four lines each, named
after the telegraph because Krklec wanted their messages short and quick.
The moral is left to the title and the last line, rather than spelled out.

*Kuma* (godmother) is the polite way animals address the fox in Croatian fables,
"kuma lisica". The pheasant is polite, and out of reach.

*Njoj će fazan* is literally "to her the pheasant will", the verb of speaking
left out, as in a story told aloud; the English keeps it out too.

*Što* here is the everyday "why" (*zašto*), not "what".

*Lija* is a short, folk word for *lisica* (fox).

Text from Lukin portal za djecu (pjesmicezadjecu.com) and lektire.me, which agree;
set in Profil Klett's school reader (hr.izzi.digital).
