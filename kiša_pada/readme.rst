#########
Kiša pada
#########

*English translation "Rain Is Falling".*

:Written by: Unknown
:Date: circa 1922

A sister finds her brother sitting sad under a tree, and asks him why.

Notes
=====

A folk song, sung slowly, every line twice; children learn it in kindergarten, and it
is sung as a lullaby. Out of copyright. It is older than the Second World War, though
often taken for a Partisan song; it was collected in Gospić, Lika (Stepanov, 1956), and a
children's version was printed in *Hrvatska revija* in 1936. The date is from Antun
Dobronić's school songbook *Pjesmarica* (1922), which has the same opening.

*Gora* is a wooded hill or mountain, in folk songs the forest itself; *gora zelena*
is "green wood". *Seka*, *braco* and *bratac* are the fond words for sister and brother.
*Jednu pticu* is "a bird", *jedan* (one) serving as "a", as it often does in speech.
*Uginuti* is "to die" said only of animals; *ona mi je uginula* is literally "it died on me", given as "it has died".

**The words vary from singer to singer**, and no single printed text was found; this
version takes its lines from the most common forms. The first two lines are as on
Lukin portal za djecu (pjesmicezadjecu.com, "Narodne pjesmice"); line 3 is as most
often remembered (the Lukin text has "pod njim sjedi moja seja, a ja pored nje", my
sister, and I beside her).

Lines 4-6 are the version where the brother is sad as his bird has died, chosen as
the gentlest for children. It rests on a single parent's memory, on the Roda parenting
forum (forum.roda.hr, 2006). Most singers, and the 1936 printing, end with a friend
killed instead: "Seka pita: Što si, braco, tako žalostan? / Imao sam prijatelja
vjerna, iskrena, / u boju je pao, pa sam žalostan" (I had a friend, faithful, true,
he fell in battle, so I am sad); others sing "Imao sam vjerna druga" or
"U ratu je poginuo" (he was killed in the war).
A kajkavian version ends quite differently, with the singer's sweetheart under the
tree (*Kajkavski zbornik*, 1974).
