#########
Uspavanka
#########

*English translation "Lullaby".*

:Written by: Zlata Kolarić-Kišur
:Date: 1957

A lullaby: the bunny sleeps under a snowy branch and dreams of summer, while the little son sleeps warm in his cradle.

===========

Notes
=====

*Nina-nana* is the sound of a lullaby, as "lullay" or "hushabye", kept as it is.

*Polagacko* is a homely form of *polagano* (gently, slowly), given as "ever so
gently". *Čedo* is an old, tender word for a child. *Zipka* is a cradle; *perina*
a feather bed. *Sipak* (here *sipki*) is powdery, of snow.

*Zeko* and *zečić* are both pet forms of *zec* (hare): one bunny, not two animals,
so both are given as "bunny". *Snježak sniježi* is literally "the little snow
snows"; it is given as "fine snow falls".

The poem rhymes in couplets (*nana* / *grana*, *spava* / *glava*); the rhymes are
not kept.

**Its author and length differ between copies.** Krunoslav Kuten's school anthology
*Hrvatski dječji pisci* (1991) prints it in full, with the source "Radost, 1957",
just before Kolarić-Kišur's "Breza" (The Birch), so it is taken to be hers; the
lyrics site tekstovi-pjesama.com also names her. Lukin portal za djecu
(pjesmicezadjecu.com, "Uspavanke") prints it as "Uspavanka" without an author, and
tekstovi-pjesama.com stops at "čedo milo", leaving out the last stanza.

Text as in Kuten, with the line breaks of Lukin portal for the first three stanzas;
the last stanza's line breaks, which the printed copy seen does not show, are set
by the rhymes and the metre of eight syllables, as in the rest. Kuten has
"svu noć sniva. Snježak sipi..." (all night he dreams. The snow sifts...) where Lukin
portal has "Svu noć sniva / Snježak sipki..."; "sipki" is kept for the rhyme with
"zipki". tekstovi-pjesama.com has "poigrat se leptirom" and Lukin portal "nježna
grana".
