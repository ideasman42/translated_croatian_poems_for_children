######################
Ševina jutarnja pjesma
######################

*English translation "The Lark's Morning Song".*

:Written by: Grigor Vitez
:Date: 1956

A skylark flies up to wake the sun, scattering song over the fields, then must come down to her chicks.


Translation
===========

- ``translated_en_gb_literal.rst`` keeps as close to the Croatian as English allows

It keeps line numbers matching the original.


Notes
=====

The skylark sings as she climbs straight up into the sky, and the poem climbs with
her, a word to a line. The short repeated lines are her song.

A *tamburica* is the small long-necked lute of Croatian folk music, kept as it is;
*zvonca* are little bells, the notes she pours down. *Ptići* are baby birds.
*Ponijeti* is to take along, and *obići* to go round and look in on, so the
English says "take along" and "look in on the chicks".

The rhymes (*dići* / *ići* / *ptići* / *sići* / *obići*) are not kept.

From *Prepelica* (Prosvjeta, 1956). Words, capitals and punctuation from Vitez's own
*Kao lišće i trava* (1960), matching Ivo Zalar (1979) and Mirko Kuten's
*Hrvatski dječji pisci* (1991); the stanza breaks, which the printed copies seen do
not show, are as on the Zagreb Faculty of Teacher Education's Vitez page
(likovna-kultura.ufzg.unizg.hr).
Kuten opens "U vis!"; the web copies have lower-case line starts.
