Kad bi drveće hodalo
####################

*English translation "If Trees Could Walk".*

:Written by: Grigor Vitez
:Date: 1959

A child imagines the trees getting up and walking: forests scattering, parks strolling, nests on the move.

Notes
=====

The title of Vitez's best-known collection (1959). Each stanza opens on the same line,
"Kad bi drveće hodalo"; the translation keeps it as one fixed line, "If the trees walked".
The title uses the more natural "If Trees Could Walk".

*Šetati* / *šetači* (to stroll / strollers) and *igrati* / *igrači* (to play / players)
pair a verb with its own noun; English can do the same, so both pairs are kept.
*Šetnje* in the third stanza is from the same family, so "strolls" rather than "walks".

*Naranča* is both the orange and the orange tree. In the last stanza the tree is asked to
walk north to a sick friend - and bring its oranges. *Nek dođe* is the jussive
"let it come": what the letter would say, kept as a bare wish.

Text from *Grigor Vitez: Izabrane pjesme* (lektire.skole.hr), except the last line,
where it prints "mog"; *moga* follows the CARNET e-textbook *Hrvatski jezik 5* and the
Zagreb Faculty of Teacher Education (likovna-kultura.ufzg.unizg.hr).
