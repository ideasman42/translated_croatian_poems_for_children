#########
Gnjavator
#########

*English translation "The Bore-ator".*

:Written by: Zvonimir Balog
:Date: 1970

A lazy bore with a blunt head lies about waiting for food to drop into his mouth, until an alligator offers to sharpen him.


Translation
===========

- ``translated_en_gb_literal.rst`` keeps as close to the Croatian as English allows

Notes
=====

Nonsense from Balog's *Nevidljiva Iva* (Mladost, 1970). *Gnjavator* is real
Croatian slang for a bore or a pest; Balog gives every noun the same *-ator* ending
(*glava*, head, becomes *glavator*; *kuća*, house, *kućator*; *škola*, school,
*školator*) until a real *-ator* turns up: the *aligator*. *Radijator* (radiator)
is a real word too, the sun's heating.

The English adds "-ator" the same way, so the joke can be read in both columns.

*Tup* is "blunt", and "dim-witted"; *zašiljiti* is to sharpen, as a pencil.
The alligator's hundred teeth say how.

*Pečeni golub* (roast pigeon) is from the saying about waiting for roast pigeons
to fall into one's mouth: expecting things without lifting a finger.

Line breaks and stanzas from Lukin portal za djecu (pjesmicezadjecu.com, "Stihovi
za 10"). The words follow the 1978 Mladost edition (a scan, read by OCR, without its
line breaks), which has "u livadi" for the portal's "na livadi" and "tvoj tupi
glavator" for "tvoj glavator", and sets the speech in dashes ("— Što mogu — govorio
je gnjavator — kad imam tup glavator").
