Narodi nam se

*English translation "Born to Us".*

One of the oldest Croatian carols: the heavenly King is born, and the new year begins with him.

Notes
=====

A traditional carol, sung everywhere at Christmas and often called the oldest
Croatian carol. Out of copyright.

*Mlado ljeto* (the young year) is the new year: *ljeto* is an old word for a year,
and the year was once counted from Christmas. The full carol has a verse for each
feast from Christmas to Epiphany, in order: Saint Stephen (26 December) follows the
birth, then Saint John, the Holy Innocents, Thomas Becket, Pope Silvester, the
Circumcision and the Three Kings. Most sing only the first verse, Saint Stephen's
and the closing wish, as here; the last two lines are the refrain, sung after each.

*Narodi nam se* is "there was born to us"; *daj nam Bog* is the old wish
"may God give us", a jussive. In the refrain, *veselimo se* and *molimo* are
read as plain present, "we rejoice", "we pray": the *mi* of the last line marks
it as a statement, and the two lines are parallel. *Veselimo se* alone could
also be "let us rejoice", as the form is the same.

The three verses as printed by the parish of Sveti Križ (zupa-svkriz.hr), matching
the 1934 *Hrvatski crkveni kantual* (on hr.wikisource.org), which gives all eleven
verses. The date is the earliest printing found, in the prayer book *Duševna okrepa*
(1861); a church paper of 1871 already names it in Marijan Jaić's carol book.
