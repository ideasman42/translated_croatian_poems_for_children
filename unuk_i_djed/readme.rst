###########
Unuk i djed
###########

*English translation "Grandson and Grandfather".*

:Written by: Grigor Vitez

A grandson tells his grandfather how easy it would be to run and jump on the Moon, and falls into a ditch.

Notes
=====

A poem of the space age, when children dreamt of rockets; the grandfather's answer is
the old one, that you must learn to walk on the ground first.

*Žuboriti* is to babble, as a brook does; the grandson ends in the water.
*Eh da* opens a wish, "oh, if only". *Sila teže* is the force of gravity.
*Tkati* (to weave) is used of telling a story, as "to spin a yarn"; *Mjesečeve
zgode* is "the Moon's happenings", the adventures he would have there.
*A nato će djed* is "and at that grandfather will", the verb of speaking left out, as in
a story told aloud. *Dajući mu ruku* is "giving him a hand", held out to pull him
from the ditch. *Po zemlji* is "on the ground", the earth underfoot, set against
the Moon.

The poem rhymes in couplets (*djedu* / *povedu*, *raketi* / *leti*); the rhymes
are not kept.
