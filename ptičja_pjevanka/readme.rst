###############
Ptičja pjevanka
###############

*English translation "The Birds' Songbook".*

:Written by: Grigor Vitez
:Date: 1956

A book falls off the roof, the birds peck up its letters, and now they sing them back in every sound a bird can make.

Notes
=====

**The bird-sound verbs are not literal.** The middle of the poem is a cascade of
onomatopoeic verbs - some real (*cvrkutati*, to chirp; *grliti* from *grlica*, the
turtle-dove's coo; *fićukati*, to whistle), many coined or dialect, built from the sounds
themselves (*ćuviču*, *žvrlje*, *ćivriču*, *biglišu*). They have no English equivalents
to be literal about, so each is given a distinct English sound-word, one for one, in
the same order. The fun is in the sound, which is best heard in the Croatian.

*Sricati* is to spell out letter by letter, as a child first learning to read;
the birds are at school. *Pitati* (to ask) is also a teacher questioning a pupil:
the sun "questions" them, then reads to them.

A *pjevanka* is a songbook, the kind used for singing lessons at school.
