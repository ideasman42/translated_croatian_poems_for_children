##############
Zeko i potočić
##############

*English translation "The Bunny and the Little Stream".*

:Written by: Branko Mihaljević
:Date: 1954

A little bunny cannot find his stream, frozen over and hidden by snow.

Notes
=====

Words and music by Branko Mihaljević (1954), and one of the best-known Croatian
children's songs. First recorded at Radio Osijek in 1956, it is best known as sung by
Zdenka Vučković (1971).

The bunny doesn't know the stream is under the snow: he thinks it has flown south
with the swallows, as birds do for the winter. *On* in the last stanza is the stream
(*potok* is masculine), not the bunny.

*Zeko* is the fond word for a hare or rabbit, "bunny". *Tam'* is *tamo* (there),
shortened for the tune; *tišti grud* (presses the breast) is an old, poetic way of
saying "troubles his heart". *Ta* is an interjection, "why" or "well".
*Visok brijeg* is "a high hill" (not "the hill is high"); the *je* before it is
"there is", given as "stands". *Nestao* is "vanished", not merely "gone".

The third stanza is the refrain. As sung it comes after the second stanza and again
after the last, and the last stanza is sung twice.

Text as on Hrvatska Wikipedia and Lukin portal za djecu (pjesmicezadjecu.com),
with the punctuation of lines 13-14 as in HRT Radio Osijek's article on the song
(2015) and tekstovi-pjesama.com. Wikipedia also gives the 1956 version, which differs
("Jedne zimske noći", "zamrz'o se potočić", "to njemu muči grud", and a last stanza
"Jer misli tužan zeko / da je potok taj / otiš'o za lastama / u dalek južni kraj").
