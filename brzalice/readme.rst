Brzalice

Notes
=====

*Brzalice* ("fast ones"), also called *jezikolomke* ("tongue-breakers"). Out of
copyright. Most are folk, of no known date; "Petar Petru plete petlju" is in print by
1870. "Cvrči, cvrči cvrčak na čvoru crne smrče" is the opening line of Vladimir Nazor's
poem "Cvrčak" (*Savremenik*, 1909), said as a tongue twister ever since. The English
gives the meaning only: the point is the sound, which can't be kept.

For an English speaker they drill the sounds of Croatian that English lacks:

- **Na vrh brda vrba mrda**: *r* serves as a vowel, a syllable on its own: *vrh*,
  *br-da*, *vr-ba*, *mr-da*. Not one of its words has an *a*, *e*, *i*, *o* or *u*
  before the *r*.
- **Cvrči, cvrči cvrčak**: *c* (as "ts" in "cats") against *č* (as "ch" in "church"),
  with *r* again as a vowel in *cvr-či*, *cvr-čak*, *cr-ne* and *smr-če*.
- **Riba ribi grize rep**: the rolled *r*.
- **Petar Petru plete petlju**: *lj* (as "lli" in "million") at the end.
- **Četiri čavčića**: the hard *č* against the soft *ć* (as "t" in "tune", said
  quickly), with *c* in *cijuču* as well.

*Mrdati* is to wiggle or twitch; a *čvor* is a knot, in wood or rope; *čavka* is a
jackdaw, *čavčić* a little one; *čun* is a small boat, *čunčić* a little one.
*Petru* is "to Petar", so Petar is plaiting a loop for another Petar (or himself).

Text from Lukin portal za djecu (pjesmicezadjecu.com, "Jezikolomke"), which gives
these among many others.
