##################
Voćka poslije kiše
##################

*English translation "The Fruit Tree after the Rain".*

:Written by: Dobriša Cesarić
:Date: 1937

A little fruit tree sparkles with raindrops in the sun, until the sun goes in.

Notes
=====

Written for adults, first printed in *Hrvatski dnevnik* (1937), then in the poet's
*Spasena svjetla* (1938), and one of the most
quoted short Croatian poems; long read in Croatian schools.
The magic is the light, not the tree.

In lines 3-4 the subject comes last: it is the *raskoš* (splendour, richness) of the
branches that glitters, lit by the sun. *Bliješti* is a strong, dazzling shine
(*blistati*, "to sparkle"), so "glitters" rather than "shines".

*Voćka* is a fruit tree, small or young; *jadno* is "poor" in the sense of
"pitiful", not "without money". *Malko* is "a little bit"; *al nek* is
*ali neka*, "but let".

The poem rhymes in couplets (*kiše* / *njiše*, *skrije* / *čarolije*);
the rhymes are not kept.

Text from Cesarić's *Slap: izabrane pjesme* (1970), with the same words in
Vlatko Pavletić's *Zlatna knjiga hrvatskog pjesništva* (1970) and other printed
anthologies. A typed reprint at the Zagreb Faculty of Humanities (ffzg.unizg.hr),
set beside a Latin translation, breaks the last line into three
("obično, / malo / i jadno drvo") and has "malo" for "malko"; web copies follow it in
part. The printed books are followed here.
